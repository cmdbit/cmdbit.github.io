Wireless networks and schools

June 30, 2025 2-minute read
Technology

I spent pretty much the whole part of 2025 optimising the wireless network in my school. Most schools don’t have the technical expertise after the initial deployment to optimise the install; they are wholly dependent on the main vendor to configure everything. I find this a hit and miss scenario - most schools are left on the default configuration and untouched after that. After all, they could always call in to the hotline to get configurations changed. Unfortunately, most wouldn’t know how to describe the problem properly and if so, users complaining about wireless will almost always complain about 1 thing : speed.

Speed is a difficult thing to troubleshoot in wifi deployments simply because of the sheer number of factors involved. People don’t seem to understand that wifi is a best effort delivery service and demand ‘instantaneous’ performance which is actually quite hilarious. Boots on the ground (and knowledgeable ones at that) are needed if school networks are to be calibrated properly for use.

Most schools are high density environments so there are some general principles to follow :-

  • All backhaul links to be on 1Gbps wired links - no mesh networks
  • Prioritise 5Ghz channels over 2.4Ghz ones.
  • 5Ghz channels are to be 40Mhz in width, 2.4Ghz to be on standard 20Mhz width
  • TX power adjustments to be at least on -3dB (1/2) and event -6dB (1/4) when APs are close by
  • Less than 6 SSIDs should be broadcasting at any one time
  • If there is an cell autosizing option, switch it on
  • If there is a channel autoselection, switch it on

These settings made the most difference to the stability of the network.

When designing networks, a rough estimate is to take the school student population and divide that by 50 to get the number of required APs. This number of APs can be placed on wifi layout planners (Ubiquiti has a mean design center to maximise coverage.

Another obvious approach is to reduce the amount of streaming traffic on the network, since most consume huge amounts of bandwidth. Culprits include Disney Plus and Netflix but even on an aggregate level, Instagram, Facebook and Tiktok consume significant amounts as well. My policy is always to block them wherever possible.
