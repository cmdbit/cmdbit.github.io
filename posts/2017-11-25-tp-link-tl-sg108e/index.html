TP-Link TL-SG108E

November 25, 2017 2-minute read
Technology

I recently purchased a TP-Link TL-SG108E to augment my VLANed home network. I had trouble understanding the terminology of TP-Link’s VLAN configuration at first. According to its manual, there were 3 types of VLANs, namely, MTU, port-based and 802.1Q VLANs. MTU VLANs was interesting as it had nothing to do with the normal meaning of MTU (maximum transmission unit) in networks at all - it stood for Multi-Tenant Unit VLAN. According to the instruction manual, MTU VLAN (Multi-Tenant Unit VLAN) defines an uplink port which will build up several VLANswith each of the other ports. Each VLAN contains two ports, the uplink port and one of the other ports in the switch, so the uplink port can communicate with any other port but other ports cannot communicate with each other Port based was severely limited in its offering, as you could do only VLANS 2-8. Although it was enabled by default, VLANs were divided by port and that wasn’t really what I wanted. This meant that I had to use 802.1Q. To trunk (in Cisco’s terminology) a port, I needed to place all the ports in tagged mode to those VLANs. The equivalent in Cisco is to issue a switchport mode trunk command. To place a port in access mode, I had to put the port in untagged mode for the relevant VLAN and then put the VLAN ID into the 802.1Q PVID page of that port.
