My thoughts on the Chromebook Pixel

February 23, 2013 2-minute read
Technology

The Chromebook Pixel was recently announced by Google. Sporting an extremely high resolution touch screen and an Intel Core i5 processor, it resembles a high end laptop with its anodized aluminium finish and clean lines. And it has a high price to match - $1299 to be exact.Despite what some reviewers mentioned, I don’t think it looks like the MacBook Pro which is probably the target laptop that it is aimed at. It has a distinctive look to it, which can’t be said for the Samsung Chromebook. The latter looks just like an exact replica of the MacBook Air.I have trouble reconciling the price of this Chromebook with its intended usage pattern. At its heart, it runs ChromeOS, the exact same OS as the Samsung Chromebook which sells for $249. At that price, you would expect the Pixel to have some offline capability comparable to laptops running other operating systems at the same price point. But no, it has the same limitations as the other machines sporting ChromeOS.That then is the dilemma. Would it be worth the high end sticker price, even with the financial value of the 1TB of Google Drive storage thrown for free for 3 years into the mix? I don’t think so. Firstly, the state of web applications at this present time is still too immature for all types of applications to be ported natively. By all types, I would include the likes of multimedia editing software such as Adobe Photoshop and Adobe Premiere. We have not reached the level of sophistication required in these applications for web applications to completely replace their offline counterparts.Another problem hinted above is offline capability. At the time of writing, Quickoffice is offered together with the purchase of the Pixel, but with only limited editing capabilities. I’ve read it will take another 2 to 3 months for Google to refine the software to the point where it can seamlessly edit the offline document and sync with the online version on Google Drive. This is only a software update, and I expect this update to be offered to other Chromebooks in the forseeable future.I am left wondering what point Google is trying to make in announcing the Chromebook Pixel. Perhaps Google is trying to state that ChromeOS is not only for low end netbooks as is the case now, but it is also for the high end market. Or perhaps Google is testing the waters with its OEMs. Whatever the case, time will tell in revealing Google’s direction and strategy for ChromeOS.
