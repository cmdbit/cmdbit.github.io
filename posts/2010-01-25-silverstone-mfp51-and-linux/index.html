Silverstone MFP51 and Linux

January 25, 2010 2-minute read
Technology • Personal

The Silverstone MFP51 is a multifunction LCD display that fits into an available 5.25" bay. I bought it over a year ago, but only got to fix it into my system yesterday.Since my OS was Linux, the provided CDs were basically useless to me. After the installation, I installed LCDproc, a Linux utility that provided LCD control for displays such as these.One of the reason why Linux proved to be an invaluable OS is the degree of verbosity in the logs. LCDproc required me to define the LCD display type in its configuration file, but nowhere on the Internet did it state what type it was. So dmesg I went … [ 21.975982] lirc_imon: Driver for SoundGraph iMON MultiMedia IR/Display, v0.6[ 21.976003] lirc_dev: lirc_register_driver: sample_rate: 0[ 21.976037] lirc_imon: Registered iMON driver (lirc minor: 0) That meant that the Silverstone MFP51 was simply a rebadged iMON display.The whole list of drivers was listed here in /etc/LCDd.conf.

The following drivers are supported:# bayrad, CFontz, CFontz633, CFontzPacket, curses, CwLnx, ea65, # EyeboxOne, g15, glcdlib, glk, hd44780, icp_a106, imon, imonlcd, IOWarrior,# irman, joy, lb216, lcdm001, lcterm, lirc, lis, MD8800, ms6931, mtc_s16209x,# MtxOrb, mx5000, NoritakeVFD, picolcd, pyramid, sed1330, sed1520, serialPOS,# serialVFD, shuttleVFD, sli, stv5730, svga, t6963, text, tyan, ula200,# xosd Link to heading

Obviously, the correct driver would either be imon or imonlcd. And after 2 trials, I found that the correct one was imonlcd.After that, I issued 2 commands to start the LCDd daemon, and then to start the LCDproc client that listens and latches on the /proc filesystem. root@test:/etc$ sudo /etc/init.d/LCDd restartroot@test:/etc$ sudo lcdproc -c /etc/lcdproc.conf And viola, some beautiful wording came onto the display. :)I haven’t tried LiRC yet, but I believe it would just be a matter of configuring the .lircrc file to make it happen.In short, the Silverstone MFP51 on Linux just works.
