Sunrays as compared to other thin clients

May 6, 2009 3-minute read
Technology • Business

Yesterday, I attended a seminar organised by one of our vendors together with a colleague. Most of the people were from the storage and virtualisation industry, as the seminar was geared mainly for the big thing in IT, virtualisation. The big players, IBM, Lenovo, VMWare, EMC and Symantec were there, and so were the smaller ones such as Datastor, Storagecraft and Novell. Each had their own particular product to market, and as expected in such a seminar, product hard-selling is quite common amongst all the booths I visited.One of these booths is this company called Wyse. Wyse markets thin clients, which puts it in a position directly competing with mine which sells thin clients as well. I was a little kay poh and decided to walk over to their booth for a little look see. The technology is quite impressive and delivers a lot of features to the consumer. And even the presenter was (in my opinion) doing a bit of Sunray bashing once he knew which company I was from.Which brings me to the point of this post. What really distinguishes the Sunray from the other thin clients such as the Wyse-s and HPs?Firstly, power consumption. All thin clients consume much less power than a normal PC, but Sunrays only consume 4 watts of power compared to around 6.6 watts for a Wyse S10 or 14.1 watts for a V10L. Doesn’t seem like much, but quickly add up all those watts in a mission-critical environment, and those watts won’t seem so small after all.Secondly, complexity. Sun only offers 3 models of thin clients (Sunray 2FS, Sunray 2, and Sunray 270), while Wyse offers many more models, 14 if I remember correctly. If there has to be some many models in the product range, there surely needs to be some level of maintenance within the machines themselves. The Sunrays on the other hand are only client display devices and thus require absolutely zero maintenance. We have a few first generation Sunrays sitting in our office and they still work as well as the second generation devices.Thirdly, in the strictest sense of the word, Wyse thin clients are not really thin clients but embedded devices. You will definitely need to patch the clients’ embedded OSes. In contrast, the Sunrays only contain enough logic to do display graphics and network, but that’s about it. No embedded WinXPe or WinCE to manage. There’s some implications to security here, but it’s probably arguable. After all, what can you do with a hacked thin terminal???I’m not bashing the Wyse thin clients, and in fact, the Sunrays have issues (isochronous USB devices such as webcams come to mind) of their own which the Wyses do not face. The Wyse thin clients, together with the TCX accompanying software, deliver quite a bit of desktop functionality, with multimedia and USB redirection.An aside at this juncture. I do see some culture shock on the part of some people when they first encounter the Sunray and its limitations but I think all that can be easily overcome through user education and other methods. Frankly, what worries me most about the Sunrays is the fact that Oracle has now bought Sun, and what its committment to Sun’s various projects, which includes the Sunray, still remains an unknown.But I’m sure (and hopeful) that the market is big enough for 4 or more thin client manufacturers … :)
