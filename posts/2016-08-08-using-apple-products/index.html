Using Apple products

August 8, 2016 3-minute read
Technology • Apple

So recently I have transitioned to using Apple products as my laptop / tablet / phone combo instead of a Chromebook / Samsung Galaxy Tablet 10.1 / Nexus 6 combination. This is due to several market shifts and personal circumstances which have shifted somewhat into Apple’s favour.

LastPass was bought over by LogmeIn; this move didn’t resonate well with the community. I looked around for alternatives, somewhat reluctantly as LastPass had one of the best browser integration in the market. One of these alternatives was 1Password; I evaluated both their Windows and Mac versions and found their Mac version superior in UI design and easier to use. 1Password also had Android and iOS versions of their phone software. What sank the deal for me was that support for their new storage format, opvault, was only on iOS and not Android. This wasn’t surprising to me as any software company, let alone AgileBits, would have to prioritize development on particular platforms.

My Nexus 6 was suffering from poor battery life. I was looking for a new phone with 2 day battery life and the Nexus 6 had around 30% by dinner time. This was with moderate screen time usage and 15 minutes of calling. In comparison, the iPhone SE which came out in March 2016, had arguably better battery life and was easier on the hands too.

I had trialled Windows 10 for 2 months prior. I truly believe Windows 10 is one of Microsoft’s best efforts to date but what wasn’t impressive was that reboots for updates were automatic on the non-Enterprise SKUs. This meant that if I had any work unsaved on the desktop, they would be lost from the reboot. I had encountered grief from doing and leaving work overnight only to find it lost the next morning. At least ChromeOS gives me a choice on whether to restart to continue the update process. I did have access to Enterprise SKUs (the LTSB version was surprisingly good) but I didn’t want to be restricted to just these.  I had accounts in both Office 365 and Google Apps. Both are great in what they do; email and collaborative tools were of no issue in both cases, and I enjoyed both immensely. In the end, Google Apps won only from a price perspective.

The iPad Pro 12.9” had come out. For me, this was the coup de grace for the form factor. For me, the tablet had come of age. The user interface, unlike the gigantic Samsung 18 incher tablet, takes advantage of the screen real estate with side by side apps to deliver a unique experience. It was noticeably bigger than the 9.7” iPad Air 2 at the time and this made using the on-screen keyboard much more bearable. With the 9.7” screen, the on-screen keyboard took up almost half the screen, leaving precious little for anything else.

I still have my Chromebook which is immensely useful for typing. Typing on a glass screen still doesn’t cut it for me for anything longer than 2 sentences.
