Google software replacements

December 16, 2012 2-minute read
Technology

I’ve been mulling around and deciding which software to use as replacements for my Google experience. The main objective around this project is to determine whether the current opensource tools are ready and have functionality equivalent to that of the Google applications. I am happy to say that for the most part, they are equivalent. A secondary aim is for privacy advocates – I am becoming increasingly disturbed over the prevalent use of tracking cookies, Google Adsense and social plugins in various websites, not least withstanding Google. And despite what has been put into the EULA, all these have the capability of tracking more than just one’s presence on the Internet. Let’s look at the list of software alternatives I concluded with in this exercise. Blogger – WordPress Google Reader – Tiny Tiny Reader GMail – Roundcube, PostFix, Dovecot Picasa – Gallery2 / Gallery3 Google Analytics – AWStats None of them were hard to set up, and with the exception of AWStats, all require only a database backend and a webserver frontend, The obvious stack to run all these was a LAMP one, with MySQL database and Apache webserver on a Centos machine. WordPress’ famous 5 minute installation truly lived up to its name, with Gallery2 coming not too far behind. The email portion was the hardest to implement, as it required manipulation of DNS records and prior knowledge of POP3/SMTP/IMAP and SPF/DKIM records. Would I recommend this for anyone? Yes, as with all things, if you are willing to dive deep into Unix / Linux and get your hands dirty, it’s a worthwhile experience. For the average person without a Unix background, no – it’s too troublesome and the steep learning curve will negate everything. It’s probably better in this case to go for a shared web hosting plan and using one of those quick installers on CPanel.
