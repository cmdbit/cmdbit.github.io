Moving away from Google's silo

May 16, 2013 2-minute read
Personal • Technology

After becoming an ardent fan of Google in the previous year, I had become more and more aware of its reach and control of data. Recent reports on its involvement in data gathering had become quite concerning to me and I was determined to wrest control of my data away from them. The last straw came when Google closed down Google Reader. This had become an important source of news for me and its closure meant that I had to look for alternatives. I decided to take this opportunity to take stock of what I had in the company and move the data away. Here were the alternatives I found. Google Drive - Dropbox Google Calendar - DaviCal and Mozilla Thunderbird Google Contacts - DaviCal and Mozilla Thunderbird Google Chrome - Mozilla Firefox Google Reader - TT-RSS reader (self-hosted) Google Maps - OpenStreetMaps Blogger - Wordpress The troublesome part was Google Reader; after searching for alternatives such as Feedly and The GoodReader, I wasn’t convinced that they were the right fit for my usage. I did a bit more digging and found TT-RSS which fit my needs perfectly. However, it requires self-hosting, installation and configuration which would be way beyond what a normal person would be capable of. This would stop me from recommending it as a alternative to Google Reader. I had been quite happy with my choices after running with them for a week or so. A lower reliance for Google services is always a good thing but this is tempered by the fact that I will need to maintain my own services.
