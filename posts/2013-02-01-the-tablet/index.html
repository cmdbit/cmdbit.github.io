The tablet

February 1, 2013 3-minute read
Technology

I recently got a new tablet – the Samsung Galaxy Note 10.1. And I am loving it.

Recently, I thought of my usage habits on my phone and tablet. While the phone had been relegated back to voice calls, my main activity had always been on my Samsung Galaxy Tab 8.9. My surfing, checking of emails and usage of apps were primarily on the tablet even though both tablet and phone were Android and could run the same applications. Data was consumed on a level 3 to 4 times that of voice. And given the amount of voice calls I make per month, I will be lucky to even exceed $5 in a single month. With that in mind, it made a lot of sense to go with a tablet with phone functionality than a phablet. The emphasis here was screen size, and I had a lot of trouble previously with a touch interface on a 4.3″ screen. At the time of purchase, the Note series from Samsung had the 5.5″ (Note 2) and the 10.1″ (Note 10.1). There were confirmed rumours of the 8″ phablet (GT-N5100) as well. However, I decided to go with the 10.1 inch form factor as it offered the best value for money (both the Note 2 and the Note 10.1 were approximately the same price) and that the note-taking functions required more screen real estate than a 8″ or 5.5″. That is not to say that the Note 10.1 is without its disadvantages. The points I covered in my earlier post are still valid. That is, typing on a touch keyboard is a utter disgrace, the UI is inconsistent between applications and I would not be seen in public talking to a 10.1 inch tablet. The last point was a sticky one. It bothered me that I needed a solution to perform quality voice calls and also listen to music from the Note 10.1 without having to reach for an unsexy 10.1 inch tablet to control the music. Quality voice calls can only be gotten through noise-cancelling microphones and in the end, I opted for a Plantronics Voyager Legend which had not 2 but 4 noise-cancelling mics. It was quite cool to walk around with the Bluetooth headset and listen to voice prompts telling that the signal was weak or that the headset had lost connection. Listening to music was another issue altogether. I need something that could also do calls, since in theory, I could be in the middle of listening to music when a call arrives. The Sony Smart Headset Pro offered such a solution as it supported the Bluetooth A2DP profile. When I tested it, the Sony headset switched between the call and the music instantly without a hitch. The buttons on it could also control the active music player in the tablet so I could now leave the tablet in the bag. To top it off, it has an inbuilt MP3 player with a removable microSD card slot – good for those times when you want to save battery on the tablet. This together with the Samsung Chromebook is an unstoppable combination.
