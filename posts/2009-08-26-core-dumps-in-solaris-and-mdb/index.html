August 26, 2009 2-minute read
Technology • Business

Today I was presented with a situation where I needed to find out why a particular system had crashed and rebooted at a customer site by itself. Server load was normal.The system had generated core dumps so I took them out. All I had to do now was to find out what was the process owning it.I copied a script (that I found on the Internet) to help with printing out the information of the threads into a file in /tmp. STORAGE# cat ./findstack.vmcore.sh#!/usr/bin/env shecho “::ps” | mdb -k /var/ak/core/unix.1 /var/ak/core/vmcore.1 | \ nawk ‘$8 !~ /ADDR/ {print $8" “$NF}’ > /tmp/.core.$$cat /dev/null > /tmp/core.$$while read ps; do echo “process name: echo ${ps} | nawk '{print $2}'” » /tmp/core.$$ echo ${ps} | nawk ‘{print $1”::walk thread | ::findstack"}’ | \ mdb /var/ak/core/unix.1 /var/ak/core/vmcore.1 » /tmp/core.$$ echo » /tmp/core.$$done \rm /tmp/.core.$$exit 0 Ahh, yes … I finally managed to pin it down. process name: smbdstack pointer for thread ffffff10d74f98e0: ffffff00109a0dc0[ ffffff00109a0dc0 _resume_from_idle+0xf1() ] ffffff00109a0df0 swtch+0x160() ffffff00109a0e50 cv_wait_sig_swap_core+0x170() ffffff00109a0e70 cv_wait_sig_swap+0x18() ffffff00109a0ec0 sigsuspend+0xeb() ffffff00109a0f10 sys_syscall32+0x101()
