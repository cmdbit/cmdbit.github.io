Virtual Private Servers

July 12, 2013 2-minute read
Technology • Personal

I had wanted to experiment with VPS solutions for quite a while now. VPS or virtual private servers is a hosted solution that have many advantages (and disadvantages) to normal web hosting solutions. They give you full root access to the server; this allows you to control the software installed on the machine and so the machine can be used for purposes other than web hosting. For example, some people use VPSes to run their Minecraft gaming server; others use them as a cheap(er) alternative to DropBox. The disadvantages of VPSes? You share the node with many other people as opposed to a dedicated server and you and you only can use. Many performance issues are a direct result of people abusing their usage of their VPS. Most hosts however do check for the existence of performance issues on their nodes and shutdown VMs that require too much CPU processing cycles or RAM. After much research, I went with a US VPS provider instead of a NZ one. Price was a major concern for me as this was for personal stuff and not work. Most local providers were charging between NZD $10 to $20 per month for an OpenVZ 512MB 20GB VM that was simply too small for my needs. I found a relatively good provider in the States that was charging only $30 per year - this price was more palatable to me. While the provider had a few bad online reviews, I decided to take the plunge. There were no more nodes available in Los Angeles so I had to settle for another location. Los Angeles is preferable for customers in the Asia/Pacific region due to lower latency.
