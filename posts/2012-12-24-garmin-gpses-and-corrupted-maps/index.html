Garmin GPSes and corrupted maps

December 24, 2012 2-minute read
Technology

I use a Garmin Nuvi 2565 to help in travelling around and that GPS has saved my rear on more than one occasion. I brought it back with me on my travels to Singapore as my family and I were going to travel up to Johore Bahru for some R&R. It was working well until I loaded a corrupted gmapsupp.img into the system main memory. Upon a reboot, the system simply refused to load and was stuck at the ‘Loading maps’ screen. Cue Google to the rescue. An hour later yield many search results. Apparently, this was a very common complaint among Nuvi owners and the advice to many newbies was to simply put the new maps onto the removable microSD card. If ever the map was well and truly corrupted, one could simply remove the microSD card. But what if the map in question was in the unit’s main memory? A person wrote a Windows utility called Garmin Cure. What the utility does is to simply take a source firmware file for the GPS and convert it into a firmware that does nothing but present a UMASS storage device to the operating system once booted up. One could then delete away the offending file and then reflash it with the correct firmware. I tried it out. I first took an old firmware for the Nuvi 2565 (version 2.90) and converted it via Garmin Cure. I then launched the GPS into preboot mode by holding on to the top left hand corner of the LCD and pressing the power button at the same time. I then launched the firmware updater script which was able to then load the firmware to the unit. Windows 7 could then see the GPS as a drive and I was able to delete away the offending file. The unit was then subsequently flashed and updated with the correct firmware. A simple and well-written utility for the Garmin GPS. A must-have for any Garmin GPS owner.
