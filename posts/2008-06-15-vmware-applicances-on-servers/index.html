VMWare Applicances on servers

June 15, 2008 2-minute read
Technology

I’m been arrowed to do a DR project based on an implementation of VMWare ESX server. One of the many interesting questions I have been posed is this : what is the optimum number of VM appliances that one can place in a single box?There is no hard and fast rule to the number of VM appliances, and as such, different people will give different answers to this question. I suppose in my case, my answer is this :-For heavy load VMs, 15 to 20 of such VMs can co-exist in a box.For small to medium load VMs, 30 to 50 of such VMs can coexist.There is no established way currently to measure the thresholds of such VMs; indeed, I believe VMware is only beginning to establish a benchmark called VMmark to benchmark VM performance. All I can say is that such numbers are only gotten through trial and error as well as gut feelings. It is hard to qualify and quantify such experiences.Put too few VMs in a box, and one purpose of implementing VMs is defeated, which is to maximise the usage of resources (which will be otherwise wasted) within that box. Put too many, and you are faced with the perennial problem of putting too many eggs in one basket. A single hardware failure will cause a ripple effect through all the appliances in that system.Virtualisation is set to be the next big thing in server computing. But only time will tell on what norms the industry will adopt, such as the number of appliances per server. And until then, I guess everyone will be fighting over this in the various projects implementing VMs.
