Switch ACLs versus Firewalls

January 7, 2008 2-minute read
Technology

I have been thinking about this topic ever since a particular issue erupted over at one of my customers’ premises during a meeting. The issue at hand was the deployment of a Layer 3 switch at a network topology where a firewall should be deployed. Should a switch with ACLs be used, or perhaps a firewall be in a better position to handle the job?

Ever since vendors started deploying multiple feature sets in their product lines, the lines drawn between switches, routers and firewalls have steadily become blurrier and blurrier. Firewalls can now do dynamic routing, and switches can even achieve packet inspection at wirespeed. Where can the line be drawn at deploying either a switch or a firewall?

For me, I am of the opinion that the following points be considered in such a scenario.

a) Performance What is the difference between throughput performance capabilities of each device, and how does that relate to its usage?

b) Logging capabilities How effective is the logging done by either device? Does performance suffer if logging is enabled?

c) Manageability

How easily can each system be maintained and updated? Will the ACL grow to be unmanageable once it reaches a certain size?

d) Cost A sensitive topic but a valid one. The point used against me was the matter of cost. Why not use an enterprise switch that costs only S$6000 when an approved firewall can reach up to S$20,000? What are the drawbacks of sacrificing performance for cost?

e) Fundamental purpose What is a switch designed to do? How about a firewall? Should we design networks with switches only to selectively broadcast traffic and not to ACL it? Firewalls on the other hand are able to perform stateful inspection of packets passing through it, and not just accept or deny traffic.

It is easy to maintain that the reason to deploy an ACL-enabled switch is purely due to cost-benefit analysis, but I have seen many bad decisions made on the premise of low budgets.

My primary concern is that the device deployed should be suitably sized for its task. An underconfigured device will kill the network quickly.

The final decision was to deploy an ACL-enabled switch. Evidently, the network topology change was initiated by their system team and they had a vested interest in it. My questioning probably pushed them into a defensive posture, and well, amicable is not really the correct word to describe the meeting.

We shall see the outcome of the meeting and the decision reached.
